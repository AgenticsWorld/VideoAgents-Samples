Plan of a 48 by 27 metre stretch of an 1860s English shingle beach in an Art Nouveau dream after Alphonse Mucha, built as a real location set. The left quarter of the image is calm pale jade-grey sea with small curling wavelets. The waterline runs from top to bottom about 30 percent from the left, in a gentle flowing curve. From the waterline to about 62 percent across lies a broad strip of firm, flat, wet sand, smooth and reflective; along its right edge a curving line of white dried foam and shell grit marks the tide-line. From there to the right edge rises a bank of rounded grey and fawn flint shingle, with sea-kale tufts near the right edge. On the upper beach, about 70 percent across and exactly at mid-height, a low flat-topped grey rock ledge about 3 metres long and 1.5 metres deep sits alone on the shingle, its top seen from above with curving flowing strata, draped bladderwrack and sea-pinks in its cracks, a small rock pool beside it. Near the top of the image a dark weathered timber groyne runs from the shingle across the sand into the sea, its posts capped with verdigris iron wave-scroll finials. On the wet sand below-left of centre two small wooden spades stand upright, nobody near them. On the shingle below-right of the ledge a few stranded lobster pots and a coil of old rope. Low golden evening sun from the west (over the sea, left) with long shadows toward the east, sparkle on the water.
