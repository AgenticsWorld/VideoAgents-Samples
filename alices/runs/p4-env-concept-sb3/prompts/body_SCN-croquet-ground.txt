Plan of the Queen's croquet-ground, a 60 by 35 metre oblong lawn in an Art Nouveau dream garden after Alphonse Mucha, built as a real set. Along the top (north) edge runs a raised honey-coloured limestone terrace, a narrow pale stone strip across the whole width, its lawn-side edge a carved balustrade seen from above as a thin line of baluster tops; at its centre a flight of stone steps descends to the lawn, and just above the steps on the terrace a crimson canvas awning on a gilded iron frame, seen from above as a crimson rectangle. The left (west), bottom (south) and right (east) sides are closed by tall clipped dark yew hedges, fully present along those edges, seen from above as thick flat-topped green bands, with a rounded arched gap cut through the hedge at the bottom-left corner and another at the bottom-right corner. The whole lawn is heaved into long regular parallel ridges and furrows running left to right, about six gently curving ridges from top to bottom, each ridge lit on one flank and shadowed in the furrow. Two small croquet stakes striped red, white and black stand at the middle of the left end and the middle of the right end. In the bottom-left corner a great cedar of Lebanon, its flat layered plates of dark foliage seen from above, casting a deep pool of shade; at the upper-right edge of that shade a green-and-gold cast-iron garden bench. A strip of herbaceous border in pastel bloom runs inside the hedges. No hoops, no balls. Hot low late-afternoon sun from the west (left) rakes across the ridges, long shadows toward the east.
