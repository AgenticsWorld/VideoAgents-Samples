Plan of a 48 by 27 metre stretch of shaded midsummer English woodland in an Art Nouveau dream designed after Alphonse Mucha. A single-file beaten-earth path about 1.2 metres wide enters at the middle of the left edge slightly below centre, curves in one flowing S-bend across the middle of the image, and reaches a fork about four fifths of the way across; from the fork one branch runs up to the upper right corner and one runs down to the lower right corner. About a quarter of the way from the left, just above the path, stands an old crab-apple tree: its rounded crown is seen from above and one thick, almost level limb with a pale worn upper surface reaches out from it and crosses over the path, clearly visible, hung with honeysuckle. At the fork stands an ivy-clad oak, its crown just above the fork, with one long branch reaching out over the fork. Just below and right of the fork a small leaning wooden fingerpost is seen from above as a post top with two short carved arms. Above the crab-apple, toward the upper left, a clear strip of bare leaf litter leads off the path into the wood. Foxgloves and dog-roses line both path edges in a flowing border; bracken and bramble cover the rest of the ground; the round crowns of grey beech and ash trees stand in loose ranks around all four edges of the image. The canopy directly over the path, the crab-apple limb and the oak branch is open, forming round gaps of light, so the whole path, both limbs, the fork and the fingerpost are fully visible from above. Late-afternoon sun from the west (left) throws dappled coins of light on the path and soft shadows toward the east.
