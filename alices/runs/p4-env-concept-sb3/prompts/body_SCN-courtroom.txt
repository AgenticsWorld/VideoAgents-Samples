Plan of the interior of a tall Art Nouveau courtroom after Alphonse Mucha, built as a real set, 20 metres long and 13 metres wide, seen from directly above with the roof not present so the whole floor is visible. The room fills most of the image as a rectangle with its long axis running left to right; all four walls are continuous thick dark oak wall lines enclosing the room. The left (south) wall line has one wide gap at its centre: the main double doorway. The right (north) wall line has a small narrow gap near its top corner: the officers' door. The top wall line and the bottom wall line are continuous with no gaps. The top (east) and bottom (west) walls are fully present along the room edges, seen only as their top edges and thickness; the floor ends at those wall lines; windows, doors, carved panels and anything mounted on a wall face are not visible from directly above and must not be drawn. The floor is a mosaic of red, black and buff tesserae with cream and gold vine borders and a large circular roundel pattern at the centre. At the right end a raised dais of three steps carries a double throne, seen from above as two tall crimson-cushioned seats side by side facing left; just left of the dais near the top corner, a low oak herald's step. Along the top wall, left of centre-right, a long raised oak jury-box with two tiers of six seats and a ledge with small slates, facing down into the room. Just below-left of the jury-box, a small raised oak witness box with a rail. Exactly in the middle of the floor a table with a large dish of tarts on it. Along the bottom wall, centre-right, a low oak dock enclosure with an iron rail. Along the left wall inside the doorway, a shallow public gallery seen from above as a narrow balcony floor with a curving verdigris cast-iron front. The open floor between is empty. Warm evening light from the high west windows falls in soft coloured patches across the floor from the bottom wall toward the top wall.
